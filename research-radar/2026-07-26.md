## What we chose to build, and why

**One artifact this week**, following the "quality over quantity" bar:
one world-class primitive beats three mediocre ones. The batch contains
`licensechain` and nothing else.

**`licensechain`** -- offline license-chain hygiene linter for AI
supply chains. Zero-dep, MIT. Direct response to the frontier signal
at [1] below: arXiv 2607.20300 ("Don't Trust the Label: License
Laundering in AI Supply Chains", 2026-07-23) traces 232,270
`dataset -> model -> application` chains and quantifies empirically
how license obligations silently drop as artifacts flow through the
pipeline -- share-alike datasets end up in permissively-licensed
models; non-commercial datasets end up in commercial ones; copyleft
libraries lose their notice/source obligations at the composition
step. `licensechain` is the small pre-publish primitive that answers
"does my chain preserve its upstream obligations?" entirely offline,
in a form that runs as a pre-commit hook, CI check, or agent tool
call. Twelve documented rules: missing license (LIC-001), unknown
SPDX id (LIC-002), parse error (LIC-003), copyleft dropped
downstream (LIC-004), notice not preserved (LIC-005), share-alike
violated (LIC-006), other incompatibility incl. no-derivatives and
RAIL propagation (LIC-007), unversioned GPL (LIC-008),
NOASSERTION / NONE (LIC-009), custom LicenseRef- (LIC-010),
non-commercial upstream in commercial downstream (LIC-011), orphan
component (LIC-012). Full SPDX 2.3 expression parser (WITH, AND, OR,
precedence, parens, LicenseRef); OR-semantics correctly interpreted
(downstream may pick any compatible branch of a dual-licensed
upstream); obligation propagation only fires when EVERY viable legal
path carries the obligation. 135 tests across parser, SPDX table,
compatibility matrix, rules, loader, CLI, and end-to-end fixtures.

The lab's hygiene-linter family already covers file-contents hygiene
(`envcheck`, `jsonlcheck`, `jwtcheck`), eval-stream hygiene
(`jsonldiff`, `jsonlsample`), and install-manifest hygiene
(`reqcheck`); `licensechain` extends the family to *license-chain*
hygiene -- one of the last hygiene surfaces where AI supply chains
still lack a small offline tool.

We considered a second artifact this cycle (a pre-flight
prompt-injection scanner motivated by arXiv 2607.20759
"IssueTrojanBench" [2] below) and declined: the brief's guidance is
1-3 artifacts with a strong preference for one excellent over
multiple mediocre, and `licensechain` warranted the full week's
attention. The injection-scanner primitive is deferred to a future
cycle with a clearer clean-room boundary from the paper's benchmark
implementation.

## Signal we noted (not built this week)

1. **Don't Trust the Label: License Laundering in AI Supply Chains**
   (arXiv 2607.20300, 2026-07-23). Empirical trace of 232,270
   dataset -> model -> application chains showing systematic license
   obligation drops across composition. The paper's framing (not its
   method, tooling, or reference code) is the direct motivation for
   `licensechain`; the paper's methodology was NOT consulted --
   licensechain is a small pre-publish primitive its framing implies
   is missing from AI supply chain tooling, not a re-implementation
   of the paper's analysis.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20300)

2. **IssueTrojanBench** (arXiv 2607.20759, 2026-07-24). Benchmark for
   evaluating AI coding agent robustness to malicious issue payloads
   across four attack categories and six delivery vectors. Implies a
   complementary small primitive: a pre-flight scanner that flags
   prompt-injection-shaped patterns (hidden-comment instructions,
   role-shift payloads, invisible-unicode steganography, bidi-override
   trickery) BEFORE an agent ingests the issue text. Deferred to next
   cycle -- the clean-room boundary from the paper's benchmark
   distribution needs more careful separation before implementation.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20759)

3. **Security Vulnerability Patterns in AI-Generated Code: A Cross-
   Model Comparative Study** (arXiv 2607.20713, 2026-07-24).
   Standardized vulnerability assessment framework mapping issues in
   LLM-generated automation scripts to CVSS, OWASP Top 10, and MITRE
   ATT&CK. Overlaps with existing tools (bandit, semgrep) at the
   primitive layer; the paper's contribution is the cross-model
   comparison rather than the detection primitive. Noted, not built.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20713)

4. **From Resource Flow to Executable Tests: Petri-Net-Guided LLM
   Test Generation for Concurrent Stateful Rust APIs**
   (arXiv 2607.21530, 2026-07-25). LLM-orchestrated concurrent-test
   generation via Petri-net intermediate representation. Requires a
   hosted-model loop to demonstrate honestly; not a small extractable
   primitive.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21530)

5. **What Bugs Do Prolog Students Write? An Empirical Taxonomy and
   Data-Driven Mutation Framework** (arXiv 2607.21193, 2026-07-25).
   LogMorph tool with 17 mutation operators weighted by observed
   student errors. Interesting as a pattern for domain-specific
   mutation testing, but Prolog-education-specific; noted as a
   signpost, not a candidate.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21193)

6. **Show HN: PR Quiz** (44726672, 94 pts). GitHub Action that
   generates a quiz from a pull request and blocks merge until the
   quiz is passed. Signal on the *"reviewers need a forcing function
   to prove they read the diff"* pain point. Interesting as a
   workflow tool but not a small offline primitive; noted.
   [GitHub](https://github.com/dkamm/pr-quiz)

7. **Show HN: Sourcebot** (44734891, 103 pts) -- agentic code search
   over an entire codebase; **Meka Agent** (44734471) --
   vision-based computer-automation agent achieving 72.7% on
   WebArena; **Terminal-Bench-RL** (44721791) -- RL infrastructure
   for terminal/coding agents. Sharp signal on the *state of
   agentic-tool packaging* but no small extractable primitive
   emerged; all three are framework-scale rather than
   primitive-scale.

8. **Advances in STV Margin Computation** (arXiv 2607.21178,
   2026-07-25). Algorithmic improvements for computing lower bounds
   on election margins for risk-limiting audits of single-
   transferable-vote elections. Testable in principle but domain-
   specific and adjacent to Vaikunth's core work; noted for
   completeness.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21178)

9. **Buzz to Boom: Detecting Message Progression Vulnerabilities in
   Electron Applications via Segmented Directed Fuzzing**
   (arXiv 2607.20698, 2026-07-24). Fuzzing framework for
   cross-process IPC in Electron apps. Framework-scale by design;
   the linter-friendly extraction ("Electron IPC surface hygiene
   check") is a much smaller project we've noted but not scoped.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20698)

10. **Zero-Flow Two-Sample Tests** (arXiv 2607.21542, 2026-07-25).
    Distribution-testing primitive; math-heavy and worth watching but
    requires a benchmark harness to demonstrate meaningfully; not
    obviously extractable as a standalone CLI this cycle.
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21542)

## Deferred to next cycles

- **`injscan`** -- offline pre-flight prompt-injection payload
  scanner motivated by IssueTrojanBench [2]. Ten proposed rules
  covering hidden-comment instructions, base64-decoded instruction
  patterns, unicode-tag steganography, zero-width joiner sequences,
  role-shift phrases ("ignore previous"), confusable characters in
  role labels, bidi-override tricks, outbound data-URIs, nested
  code-fence role-play, and whitespace exfiltration channels.
  Testable, world-class-buildable, complements the lab's hygiene
  family; the clean-room boundary from the paper's benchmark
  distribution needs closer thought before implementation.
- **`electronipccheck`** -- lint for Electron IPC surface hygiene
  (unfiltered messages between renderer and main, missing
  nodeIntegration flags, contextIsolation gaps). Extracts a
  primitive from [9] above.
- **`ppatern`** -- Prolog-education mutation-pattern registry
  extracted from [5] above; deferred as too domain-specific.
- **Merge-conflict-marker lint for requirements.txt** (holdover from
  prior weeks) -- a 2-hour extension composing with `reqcheck`; will
  ship when a real user needs it.

## Methodology this cycle

- arXiv `export.arxiv.org/api/query` for cs.SE, cs.PL, cs.CR,
  cs.LG-filtered-by-`abs:evaluation`, sorted by submitted-date
  descending; ~90 titles across the 2026-07-20 -> 2026-07-26 window.
- HN Algolia `search_by_date` for `"Show HN"` in the same window
  above 80 points; ~5 substantial dev-tool hits, mostly
  framework-scale.
- Triage filter unchanged from prior weeks: *can a small clean-room
  implementation actually be tested this run, without a hosted-model
  dependency?* Of ~90 arXiv titles surveyed and ~5 HN candidates,
  one (2607.20300) directly implied a small primitive we could ship
  at world-class quality this run. The rest are noted or deferred.
